## The difference between projects and areas

An **area** represents an ongoing part of your life, or a role you play. Like "work", "family" or "marketing".
A **project** on the other hand, is finite. It has a clear outcome in mind and you're working towards a 
day where you can mark the project complete and close it.

## Creating new projects and areas

You can create a new project or area from the File menu, or from + button at the bottom of the sidebar. 
When you hover over that +, a menu will pop up.

## Organising tasks in projects and areas

At first glance, a project or area might look exactly like the inbox -- but that is not the case.
One superpower that projects and areas have is [groups](/reference/glossary#group). Groups allow you to create structure.
A group has a name, and you can drag tasks into it. (These are *task groups* — distinct from the planning groups you create in a quarterly plan.)

<Frame caption="Groups in a project">
  <img src="/images/tasks/prar-groups.png" alt="Tasks organised into named groups inside a project" />
</Frame>

## Convert a group or task into a project

Work doesn't always announce itself as a project. A group inside an area fills up until it clearly
needs its own home; a single task turns out to be a fortnight's work. Rather than rebuilding it by
hand, convert it.

**From a group** — right-click the group (on iPhone, tap its `…`) and choose **Convert to Project**.
The group's name becomes the project's name, every task inside it moves across in the same order, and
the new project joins the same area the group was in. Repeating tasks that were filing themselves into
that group carry on filing into the project. The empty group is then removed.

**From a task** — select a single task and choose **Convert to Project** from its `…` menu. This one
travels light: the project takes the task's name and notes, and nothing else.

<Warning>
  Converting can't be undone. From a task in particular, the tags, schedule, priority and any calendar
  events are discarded and the original task is deleted — so if a task carries detail you need, move it
  into a group first and convert that instead.
</Warning>

## Project progress and completion

As you check tasks off, you'll notice the progress pie fills up. This provides a visual indicator
of the progress of your project. 

<Frame caption="The progress pie fills up as you check tasks off">
  <img src="/images/tasks/prar-progress.png" alt="A project's progress pie partially filled as tasks are completed" />
</Frame>

You can click the progress pie to complete (or un-complete) the project. If there are still open tasks, 
you'll be asked if you are sure. 

<Frame caption="Click the progress pie to complete the project">
  <img src="/images/tasks/prar-done.png" alt="Clicking the progress pie to mark a project complete" />
</Frame>

## Project deadlines

You can add a deadline to a project. When you do this and the deadline is near (less than 30 days away) it will show up in your sidebar to remind you. The week of the deadline, the project will also show up in your Week screen, and on the day of the deadline, on your Day screen.

## Area emoji

Areas can have an emoji, to give them some colour. You can set an emoji using the &hellip; menu or (on Mac) by
clicking the icon. Double clicking the icon removes the emoji.

<Frame caption="Click the area icon to select an emoji">
  <img src="/images/tasks/prar-emoji.png" alt="Choosing an emoji for an area" />
</Frame>

## Availability

Projects and areas are available on both **Mac** and **iPhone**.
