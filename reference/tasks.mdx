A **task** is the atomic unit of work in Sonto. Everything you do flows through tasks — capturing them,
scheduling them, and checking them off.

## Adding tasks

Click or tap the purple `+` button to add a task. On Mac you can also press <kbd>Cmd</kbd> + <kbd>N</kbd>.
The new task appears at the top of the list, ready for you to type.

<Frame caption="Adding a new task">
  <img src="/images/tasks/new.png" alt="Adding a new task with the purple plus button" />
</Frame>

### Drop a task exactly where you want it

If the top of the list isn't where the task belongs, don't add it and then move it — place it as you
create it. Press and hold the `+`, drag it into the list, and the list opens a gap to show you where
the task will land. Let go, and the task appears there with its name field already active.

You can drop into a group as well as the main list. On iPhone, either drag the `+` or hold it still
for a moment to pick it up. Dragging back onto the `+` cancels — it turns into a ✕.

This works on the inbox, day, week, project, area and tag screens. It isn't available on search,
archive or repetitions, or on a day or week that has already passed.

## Type tasks naturally

You can set a task up as you name it. Type a date, a time, a project or area, and tags right into the
name, and Sonto files them for you — `Prepare deck #Work @urgent tomorrow 3pm` schedules the task for
tomorrow at 3 pm, files it in **Work**, and tags it **urgent**, leaving the name as *Prepare deck*. See
[Natural language input](/reference/natural-language-input) for everything it understands.

## Editing tasks

To begin editing a task, double-click it (or tap it on iPhone), or select it with the arrow keys and
press <kbd>Return</kbd>. The buttons at the bottom-right let you take quick action — move it, schedule
it, or reach many more actions through the &hellip; menu.

<Frame caption="Moving a task">
  <img src="/images/tasks/move.png" alt="The quick-action buttons on a task, including move and schedule" />
</Frame>

<Info>
  You can do almost everything to a task from the keyboard. See
  [Keyboard shortcuts](/reference/other-topics/keyboard-shortcuts).
</Info>

## Reordering tasks

Pick a task up and drag it where you want it. On Mac you can do the same from the keyboard: select a
task and press <kbd>Cmd</kbd> + <kbd>&uarr;</kbd> or <kbd>Cmd</kbd> + <kbd>&darr;</kbd> to move it one
place at a time, or <kbd>Cmd</kbd> + <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>&uarr;</kbd> /
<kbd>Cmd</kbd> + <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>&darr;</kbd> to send it straight to the top or bottom.

A multiple selection moves as one block, and a task can travel from one group into the next.

<Info>
  While you're editing a task's name, <kbd>Cmd</kbd> + <kbd>&uarr;</kbd> and
  <kbd>Cmd</kbd> + <kbd>&darr;</kbd> belong to the text field instead. Press <kbd>Return</kbd> to
  finish editing first.
</Info>

Reordering applies to the inbox, day, week, project, area and tag screens. Search, archive,
repetitions and the plan screens sort themselves.

## Copying and pasting tasks

Tasks go on the clipboard like anything else — copy one in a project and paste it into next week, or
duplicate a task you set up carefully rather than building it again.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Mac">
    Select one or more tasks and press <kbd>Cmd</kbd> + <kbd>C</kbd> to copy, <kbd>Cmd</kbd> +
    <kbd>X</kbd> to cut, or <kbd>Cmd</kbd> + <kbd>V</kbd> to paste into the list you're in.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="iPhone & iPad">
    Copy, cut and paste arrive on iPhone and iPad in Sonto 2.0. Select a task and use the copy and
    paste actions in its menu.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

A pasted task brings its name, notes, tags, priority and completed state with it. It does **not** bring
its project, area or schedule — where it lands is decided entirely by where you paste it.

You can also paste plain text from another app: Sonto makes one task per line, and strips list markers
like `-`, `*` and `1.` as it goes. A line starting `[x]` arrives already completed. Copying the other
way gives you a Markdown checklist, one `- [ ] Task name` per line.

## Deleting tasks

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Mac">
    Select one or more tasks and press <kbd>Backspace</kbd> or <kbd>Cmd</kbd> + <kbd>Backspace</kbd>,
    or choose **Delete** from a task's `…` menu.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="iPhone & iPad">
    Open the task and choose **Delete** from the `…` menu at the bottom of the editor, or pick the task
    up and drop it on the trash that appears in the action bar.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

Sonto doesn't ask you to confirm — not for a task, and not for a group and everything inside it.
[Undo](#undo-and-redo) brings it back instead, along with any
[calendar event](/reference/calendar#deleting-a-task-with-an-event) the task was linked to.

## Undo and redo

Almost anything you do to your tasks can be undone: deleting, cutting and pasting, completing,
scheduling, moving between lists, reordering, renaming, editing notes, tag changes, creating a project
or an area — and the changes an [AI assistant](/reference/ai-with-mcp) makes on your behalf.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Mac">
    <kbd>Cmd</kbd> + <kbd>Z</kbd> undoes and <kbd>Cmd</kbd> + <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>Z</kbd> redoes.
    **Edit** &rarr; **Undo** names the step you're about to reverse — *Undo Delete Task* — so you can
    see what it will do before you do it.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="iPhone & iPad">
    Tap the `…` in the action bar and choose **Undo**. It's there only when there's something to undo,
    with **Redo** beside it once you've undone something.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

Undo is per-device and per-session. It doesn't travel over [sync](/reference/sync), so it only ever
reverses changes you made on the device you're holding, and the history starts empty each time you open
Sonto.

<Info>
  A few less common actions clear the undo history, so **Undo** is occasionally unavailable. That's
  deliberate: Sonto would rather offer you nothing than undo the wrong thing.
</Info>

## Turning a task into a project

Some tasks grow. When one turns out to be a piece of work in its own right, select it and choose
**Convert to Project** from its `…` menu. Sonto creates a [project](/reference/projects-areas) with the
task's name and notes, and opens it.

<Warning>
  Converting keeps only the **name** and **notes**. The task's tags, schedule, priority and any linked
  calendar events are discarded, and the task itself is deleted. If that isn't what you wanted,
  [undo](#undo-and-redo) puts the task back as it was.
</Warning>

The option appears only when you have exactly one task selected. A whole group can become a project
too, and that route keeps much more — see
[Convert a group or task into a project](/reference/projects-areas#convert-a-group-or-task-into-a-project).

## What a task can hold

A single task can carry all of the following:

- **Priority** — mark a task as priority so it stands out and surfaces on your day
  (<kbd>Cmd</kbd> + <kbd>T</kbd>). You can also type `!!!` in the name — see
  [Natural language input](/reference/natural-language-input#priority).
- **Scheduling** — assign a task to a **day** or a **week**, or place it on the
  [calendar](/reference/calendar) as a time block.
- **Notes & checklist** — context, links, formatting, and sub-tasks. See
  [Notes & Checklists](/reference/notes).
- **Project or area** — file a task in a [project or area](/reference/projects-areas), or leave it in
  the [inbox](/reference/screens).
- **Tags** — label a task so it shows up across [tag filters](/reference/tags)
  (<kbd>Cmd</kbd> + <kbd>Shift</kbd> + <kbd>T</kbd>).
- **Deadline** — a task inherits the deadline of its [project](/reference/projects-areas), which Sonto
  surfaces as the date approaches.

## Task states

A task moves through three states:

- **Open** — not started.
- **In progress** — currently being worked on. Toggle with <kbd>Cmd</kbd> + <kbd>R</kbd>, or long-press
  the checkbox.
- **Complete** — done. Toggle by clicking the checkbox or pressing <kbd>Cmd</kbd> + <kbd>E</kbd>.

## Repeating tasks

Any task can repeat — on a fixed schedule or when you complete it — and optionally add itself to your
calendar. See [Repeating tasks](/reference/repeating-tasks).

  <img src="/images/tasks/recurring.png" alt="The Edit Repetition dialog for a task" />
</Frame>

## Focus

To work on a task distraction-free, open it in [Focus](/reference/focus) — full screen, with everything
else out of the way (<kbd>Cmd</kbd> + <kbd>.</kbd> on Mac).

## Availability

Tasks work on both **Mac** and **iPhone & iPad**. The keyboard shortcuts above are Mac-only.
