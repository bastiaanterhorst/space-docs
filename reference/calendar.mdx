## Connecting your calendars

Sonto uses the calendar accounts already set up on your Mac, iPhone and iPad. Because it reads from your
device's native calendar, practically every calendar works — **iCloud, Google, Microsoft Outlook
(Exchange), Yahoo, Proton, AOL, and any CalDAV calendar**.

The first time, Sonto asks permission to use your calendars — choose **Allow Full Access**. Then click
the **+** to add any calendar on your device.

<Info>
  Calendars come from the accounts in your device's system settings — on Mac, **System Settings →
  Internet Accounts**; on iPhone, **Settings → Apps → Calendar → Accounts**. Any account there with
  calendars enabled shows up in Sonto automatically; there's no separate sign-in inside Sonto.
</Info>

### Where events go

One calendar is your **default** — new events and scheduled tasks go there. Change it in your calendar
settings. An [area](/reference/projects-areas) can also have its own default calendar, so tasks you
schedule from that area land on a specific calendar, shown in its own colour.

### Private events on Google

Events Sonto adds to your calendar are marked **private**, so anyone with access sees only "Busy". For
**Google** calendars, connect your Google account in Sonto settings (**Connect Google account**) to
enable this — the standard Apple connection can't mark Google events private on its own.

## Calendar on the Day and Week screens

On the day and week screens, Sonto shows your events right alongside your tasks, so your meetings and
your planned work live in one view.

You can create and edit events, as well as accept or decline invitations, without leaving Sonto.
You can also join video calls, either by opening the event and clicking the **Join Video Call** button,
or, on Mac, by hovering over the event and pressing <kbd>Cmd</kbd> + <kbd>J</kbd>.

## Tasks on your calendar

You can drag tasks onto your calendar. This creates an event which is linked to your task — a *time
block*. The event syncs to your calendar, so others with access to your calendar can also see it. The
event is marked **private**, so they only see "Busy", rather than the full name and notes for your task.

<Frame caption="A task on your calendar">
  <img src="/images/tasks/calendar-task.png" alt="A task dragged onto the calendar, shown as a linked time block" />
</Frame>

<Tip>
  You can also create a time block by typing. Name a task `Review deck tomorrow 3pm 1h` and Sonto blocks
  the time for you — see [Natural language input](/reference/natural-language-input).
</Tip>

Sonto can also place a task for you automatically, finding an opening that respects your working hours
and existing commitments — see [Auto-schedule](/reference/auto-schedule).

### Deleting a task with an event

[Deleting a task](/reference/tasks#deleting-tasks) takes its time block with it, and
[undo](/reference/tasks#undo-and-redo) puts the event back. The restored event returns to the same
calendar at the same time, but it is a new event: a custom alarm you added and the event's location
don't come back with it.

<Info>
  A meeting isn't Sonto's to delete. If the task is linked to an event that has other attendees, sits on
  a read-only or subscribed calendar, or is organised by somebody else, deleting the task only
  **unlinks** it — the event stays in your calendar and nobody is sent a cancellation.
</Info>

## Availability

Calendars work on both **Mac** and **iPhone**. Joining a video call with <kbd>Cmd</kbd> + <kbd>J</kbd>
is a Mac shortcut; on iPhone, open the event and tap **Join Video Call**.
